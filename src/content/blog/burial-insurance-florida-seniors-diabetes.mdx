Diabetes is one of the most common health conditions among Florida seniors, and also one of the most common reasons people assume they've lost access to life insurance entirely. That assumption is usually wrong — diabetes covers a wide range of situations, and burial insurance has real, accessible options for most of them.

## Why diabetes isn't a single answer

Type 1 and type 2 diabetes are treated differently by most underwriting criteria, and even within type 2, how long you've had the diagnosis, how well it's controlled, and whether there are related complications all matter. A senior with well-managed type 2 diabetes and no complications is in a very different position than someone with poorly controlled diabetes and related health issues — which is exactly why there's no single yes-or-no answer to "can I get burial insurance with diabetes."

## The two paths for applicants with diabetes

**Simplified issue** policies ask specific health questions, and diabetes is commonly one of them. Depending on your specific answers — type, how long you've had it, how it's managed, whether there are complications like neuropathy or vision issues — you may still qualify, sometimes at standard pricing, sometimes at an adjusted rate class.

**Guaranteed issue** policies skip health questions entirely, including anything related to diabetes. If your specific situation doesn't fit simplified issue criteria, guaranteed issue remains available, with eligibility based on age and Florida residency instead.

## Factors that often matter most

| Factor | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Type 1 vs. type 2 | Often evaluated differently in underwriting |
| How long since diagnosis | Longer-term stable management is often viewed more favorably |
| A1C control | Well-controlled blood sugar is generally viewed more favorably than poorly controlled |
| Complications | Neuropathy, vision issues, or kidney involvement can affect eligibility |
| Insulin use | Insulin-dependent diabetes may be evaluated differently than diet or oral medication-managed cases |

None of these guarantee a specific outcome on their own — actual eligibility depends on the specific product and carrier's criteria — but they reflect the general pattern.

## Why guaranteed issue is a reliable fallback

If simplified issue doesn't work out based on your specific diabetes history, guaranteed issue was built for exactly this kind of situation. It typically costs somewhat more per dollar of coverage than simplified issue, and often includes a graded death benefit for the first two to three years, meaning a beneficiary generally receives a return of premiums paid if death occurs from natural causes during that window. But it removes diabetes — and any other health condition — from the conversation entirely.

## What Florida seniors with diabetes often get wrong

**Assuming diabetes automatically means guaranteed issue only.** Plenty of seniors with well-managed diabetes actually qualify for simplified issue and better pricing, but never check because they assumed the worst going in.

**Underestimating how much "well-managed" matters.** Two people with the same diagnosis but very different levels of control can see genuinely different outcomes — which is part of why generic information can only take you so far.

**Waiting because they assume it'll be complicated.** The application process itself isn't more complicated for diabetic applicants — it's the same handful of questions, just with different answers.

## How to actually find out where you stand

Because diabetes covers such a range of situations, the only reliable way to know your real eligibility is to go through the quote process. Your specific combination of diagnosis history, management, and any complications is what actually determines your options — not a general description of "having diabetes."

## A note on lifestyle management and eligibility

Many Florida seniors with diabetes have spent years actively managing their condition through diet, exercise, medication, and regular checkups. This kind of consistent, long-term management is generally viewed more favorably than a recent or unstable diagnosis, even though both fall under the same general "diabetes" label. If you've been managing your condition well for an extended period, it's worth going through the actual quote process rather than assuming a diagnosis from years ago still limits your options the way it might have when it was first made.

## Frequently asked questions

**Does type 1 diabetes make simplified issue impossible?**
Not necessarily — it depends on the specific product's criteria and your individual management history. It's worth checking rather than assuming.

**Will I need to disclose my A1C level or provide medical records?**
Simplified issue applications are typically based on your answers to health questions rather than a medical record review, though this varies by product and carrier.

**Does diabetes affect my coverage amount options, not just eligibility?**
Coverage amount choices are generally available regardless of health history — what changes is which policy type and rate class you're likely to qualify for.

**Can complications from diabetes, like neuropathy, affect my options separately from the diabetes itself?**
Yes — complications are often considered alongside the primary diagnosis and can affect which path fits your situation.

**Is there a benefit to applying before diabetes progresses further?**
Since diabetes can be progressive for some people, and age also affects pricing, applying sooner rather than waiting is a common approach among Florida seniors managing this condition.

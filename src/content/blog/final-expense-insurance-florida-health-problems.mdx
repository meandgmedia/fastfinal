A lot of Florida residents assume that once you've been diagnosed with something — diabetes, heart disease, anything chronic — the door closes on life insurance entirely. It's one of the most common reasons people put off final expense insurance for years longer than they need to. The short answer: health problems usually don't shut you out. They just shift which type of policy makes sense.

## The two paths, and how health factors into each

**Simplified issue** policies ask a short list of health questions. Some conditions affect your rate class or eligibility for this specific path, but plenty of manageable, well-controlled conditions don't disqualify you outright — they just mean your pricing reflects your health history.

**Guaranteed issue** policies skip health questions entirely. Your health history isn't part of the equation at all — approval is based on factors like age and Florida residency, not your medical history. This exists specifically for people whose health might make simplified issue or traditional life insurance harder to qualify for.

## Common conditions and how they typically factor in

| Condition | Typical situation |
|---|---|
| High blood pressure, managed | Often still eligible for simplified issue |
| Type 2 diabetes, managed | Often still eligible for simplified issue |
| Heart disease | Depends on specifics — worth checking both simplified and guaranteed issue |
| COPD | May point toward guaranteed issue depending on severity |
| Cancer history | Often depends on how long ago and current status — guaranteed issue is a reliable fallback |

This table is general context, not a guarantee of eligibility for any specific condition — the only way to know for certain is to actually go through the quote and application process.

## Why guaranteed issue exists

If simplified issue doesn't work out because of your specific health history, guaranteed issue is built as the safety net. It typically costs more per dollar of coverage and may include a graded death benefit for the first two to three years — meaning if death occurs from natural causes during that window, beneficiaries typically receive a return of premiums paid rather than the full amount. But it removes health from the equation completely, which for many people is a fair tradeoff for guaranteed access to coverage.

## What not to do

**Don't assume you're disqualified without checking.** A huge number of people who'd actually qualify for simplified issue never find out, because they assumed guaranteed issue (or nothing) was their only option.

**Don't leave out health information to try to get better pricing.** For simplified issue applications, accuracy matters — it's how you end up correctly priced from the start rather than running into issues later.

**Don't give up after one no.** If you don't qualify for one product, that doesn't mean final expense insurance is off the table entirely — it usually just means a different product fits better.

## How to actually find out where you stand

The only reliable way to know what you qualify for is to go through the quote process itself, since eligibility depends on the specific combination of your age, your answers, and the product. Guessing ahead of time usually just adds stress without giving you a real answer.

## Frequently asked questions

**Will I be asked to prove my health conditions with medical records?**
Simplified issue applications are typically based on your answers to health questions, not medical record review, though this can vary by product and carrier.

**Is there a condition that disqualifies someone from every type of final expense insurance?**
Guaranteed issue products are specifically designed not to ask health questions, so they remain an option even for applicants who might not qualify elsewhere.

**Does my age matter more than my health condition?**
Both matter, but they affect different things — age affects pricing broadly, while health primarily affects eligibility and pricing for simplified issue specifically.

**Can I apply for both simplified and guaranteed issue to compare?**
You can compare your options during the quote process without needing to submit separate full applications for each.

Heart disease is one of the most common reasons Florida residents assume they've been shut out of life insurance entirely — and it's one of the most common assumptions we hear that turns out to be wrong. Heart disease covers a genuinely wide range of situations, from a decades-old, well-managed condition to something more recent and serious, and your options depend heavily on which end of that range you're actually on.

## Why "heart disease" isn't one single answer

The phrase covers a lot of ground: high cholesterol managed with medication, a heart attack decades ago with no recurrence, a recent bypass surgery, congestive heart failure, arrhythmia, and more. Each of these carries different weight in an insurance application, which is exactly why there's no single yes-or-no answer to "can I get coverage with heart disease." The honest answer is: it depends on specifics that only your actual application can sort out.

## The two paths, and how heart disease factors into each

**Simplified issue** policies ask a short list of health questions, and heart disease specifically is one of the more commonly asked-about conditions. Depending on how long ago your diagnosis or event was, how well it's currently managed, and whether there have been recent complications, you may still qualify — sometimes at a standard rate, sometimes at a different rate class.

**Guaranteed issue** policies skip health questions entirely, including anything related to heart disease. If your specific history doesn't fit simplified issue criteria, guaranteed issue remains available regardless of your cardiac history, with eligibility based on factors like age and Florida residency instead.

## Situations that often still qualify for simplified issue

While every carrier's specific criteria differ, these situations commonly still have a path to simplified issue pricing:

- **Managed high cholesterol or hypertension** without a cardiac event
- **A heart attack or cardiac event several years in the past**, with no recent complications and stable follow-up care
- **Controlled arrhythmia** managed with medication and no recent hospitalizations
- **Stents placed years ago** with no subsequent cardiac events

## Situations more likely to point toward guaranteed issue

- **A cardiac event within the past year or two**
- **Congestive heart failure**, particularly if recently diagnosed or unstable
- **Multiple recent hospitalizations** related to heart health
- **Pending cardiac procedures or ongoing evaluation**

This isn't an exhaustive or authoritative list — actual eligibility depends on the specific product and carrier — but it reflects the general pattern: the more recent and active the condition, the more guaranteed issue becomes the realistic starting point.

## Why guaranteed issue exists for exactly this situation

Guaranteed issue final expense insurance was built specifically for people whose health history — heart disease very much included — might make traditional underwriting difficult. It typically costs somewhat more per dollar of coverage and often includes a graded death benefit for the first two to three years, meaning a beneficiary generally receives a return of premiums paid if death occurs from natural causes during that window. But it removes your cardiac history from the conversation entirely, which for many people is a fair tradeoff for guaranteed access to coverage.

## What not to do if you have heart disease

**Don't assume you're automatically disqualified.** A significant number of people with well-managed heart conditions actually qualify for simplified issue and never find out because they assumed the worst.

**Don't leave details off a simplified issue application to try to improve pricing.** Accuracy matters — it's how you end up correctly priced from the outset rather than running into complications down the line.

**Don't stop at one no.** If one product doesn't work out, that doesn't mean coverage is unavailable altogether — it usually just means a different product, like guaranteed issue, is the better fit.

## How to actually find out where you stand

Because heart disease covers such a wide range of situations, the only reliable way to know your real eligibility is to go through the quote process itself. Generic information — including this article — can outline the general pattern, but it can't tell you definitively whether your specific history qualifies for simplified issue pricing.

## Frequently asked questions

**Will I need to provide medical records about my heart condition?**
Simplified issue applications are typically based on your answers to health questions rather than a medical record review, though this can vary by product and carrier.

**Does a family history of heart disease affect my eligibility, even without a personal diagnosis?**
This depends on the specific health questions asked, which vary by product — family history and personal diagnosis are often treated differently.

**Is guaranteed issue always the fallback for heart disease specifically?**
Not necessarily — many well-managed cardiac histories still qualify for simplified issue. Guaranteed issue is the reliable fallback if simplified issue doesn't work out, not the default assumption.

**Can my cardiac history change my coverage amount options, not just my eligibility?**
Coverage amount choices are generally available regardless of health history — what changes is which policy type and rate class you qualify for.

**How do I know if I should apply for simplified issue or go straight to guaranteed issue?**
Comparing both during the quote process is the most reliable way to find out, rather than guessing based on general information alone.

If the words "medical exam" have ever stopped you from even looking into burial insurance, you're not alone — and you're also working off outdated assumptions. Most burial insurance in Florida today doesn't require one. No nurse at your kitchen table, no blood draw, no waiting two weeks for lab results to come back before you find out if you're approved. That's not a marketing gimmick; it's actually how most of this product category works.

This guide walks through exactly how no-medical-exam burial insurance works in Florida, who it's built for, what it costs, and how to know if it's the right fit before you apply.

## What "no medical exam" actually means

When a burial insurance product is marketed as "no medical exam," it means exactly that: no physical exam, no blood work, no urine sample, none of the underwriting steps traditional life insurance often requires. Instead, Florida burial insurance without a medical exam generally falls into one of two categories.

**Simplified issue** policies still ask a short list of health questions — things like recent hospitalizations, certain diagnoses, or major ongoing treatment. There's no exam, but your answers to these questions affect both your eligibility and your price.

**Guaranteed issue** policies skip health questions entirely. There's nothing to answer, nothing to disclose, and nothing that can get you turned down based on your health history. Your age and Florida residency are essentially the whole conversation.

Both are still real, regulated, permanent life insurance — just structured to make the approval process faster and less invasive than what people often picture when they think "life insurance application."

## Why this exists in the first place

Burial insurance is designed to cover a specific, well-defined need: funeral, burial, or cremation costs, typically in the $5,000 to $25,000 range. Because the coverage amount is smaller and more predictable than a large traditional life insurance policy, carriers can afford to simplify — or in guaranteed issue's case, eliminate — the underwriting process. It's not that the industry got generous. It's that the product itself was built around a narrower, more predictable risk.

## Who qualifies for no-medical-exam burial insurance

Qualification depends on which path you're looking at.

For **simplified issue**, eligibility depends on your answers to the health questionnaire. Many common, well-managed conditions — controlled high blood pressure, managed diabetes, and similar — don't automatically disqualify you. More serious or recent health events may point you toward a different rate class or toward guaranteed issue instead.

For **guaranteed issue**, eligibility is essentially universal within the product's age range, which commonly spans somewhere between 45 and 85, though this varies by carrier. No health questions means no health-based disqualification.

Florida residency is a factor for both, since coverage availability is tied to where you live and which products are approved for sale in your state.

## What it typically costs

Pricing for no-medical-exam burial insurance depends on a handful of concrete factors, not guesswork:

| Factor | Effect on price |
|---|---|
| Age | Younger applicants generally pay less |
| Tobacco use | Tobacco users typically pay more |
| Coverage amount | Higher coverage means higher premiums |
| Policy type | Guaranteed issue often costs more per dollar of coverage than simplified issue |

Simplified issue tends to be the more affordable route if your health history qualifies you, since the carrier has more information to price around. Guaranteed issue costs somewhat more, reflecting the fact that the carrier is accepting applicants without any health screening at all.

## The graded death benefit — what it means for you

Many guaranteed issue burial insurance policies include something called a graded death benefit, typically covering the first two to three years of the policy. If death occurs from natural causes during that window, your beneficiary generally receives a return of the premiums paid, often with interest, rather than the full coverage amount. Accidental death is usually covered at the full benefit from day one, and after the grading period ends, the policy pays out in full for any cause of death.

This isn't a hidden catch — it's standard, disclosed practice across the guaranteed issue industry, and it's the mechanism that makes truly no-questions-asked approval possible in the first place.

## How to decide between simplified issue and guaranteed issue

A few honest questions can help:

1. **Do you have a health condition you'd rather not detail on an application?** Guaranteed issue skips that conversation entirely.
2. **Are you in reasonably good health with no major recent events?** Simplified issue may offer better pricing.
3. **Is speed and certainty of approval your top priority?** Guaranteed issue removes any uncertainty tied to health questions.
4. **Is minimizing your monthly premium the priority?** Simplified issue is often the more affordable path if you qualify.

There's no wrong answer here — it comes down to what matters most for your situation.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is no-medical-exam burial insurance the same as "instant" coverage?**
Not exactly. No exam means no physical screening, but the application and underwriting process still takes some time to complete, even if it's much faster than traditional life insurance.

**Can I be declined for guaranteed issue burial insurance?**
Eligibility is based on factors like age and Florida residency rather than health, so it's built to be broadly accessible, though it's not automatically guaranteed for every possible circumstance.

**Does skipping the exam mean lower-quality coverage?**
No — it's still real, permanent life insurance. The simplified process reflects the product's smaller, more predictable coverage amount, not a lower standard of coverage.

**How long does approval typically take without an exam?**
This varies by product and carrier, but many applicants see a decision far faster than the weeks a traditional, fully underwritten policy might take.

**Can I apply for both types to compare?**
You can review your options for both during the quote process without committing to either until you're ready to apply.

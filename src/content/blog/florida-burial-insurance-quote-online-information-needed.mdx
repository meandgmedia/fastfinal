One of the biggest reasons people put off getting a burial insurance quote is a vague fear that it's going to require digging through filing cabinets and old paperwork. It doesn't. Here's exactly what information actually goes into a Florida burial insurance quote, and why each piece matters.

## The short list of what you actually need

**Your date of birth.** Age is the single biggest factor in burial insurance pricing, so this is the starting point for any real quote.

**Whether you use tobacco.** Tobacco users typically see different pricing than non-users, so this affects which numbers you're shown.

**The coverage amount you're considering.** Most people have at least a rough sense of this — somewhere between $5,000 and $25,000 covers what most applicants are looking for.

**Basic contact information.** Enough for the quote platform to follow up if you choose to move forward, though this doesn't commit you to anything.

That's genuinely the core list for an initial quote. No income statements, no bank information, no medical records required just to see numbers.

## What gets added if you move toward an application

If you decide to actually apply after reviewing your quote, a bit more comes into play:

- **Answers to health questions**, if you're applying for a simplified issue product
- **Your Social Security number**, standard for any insurance application
- **Beneficiary information** — who you want to receive the benefit

Guaranteed issue applications skip the health questions entirely, since eligibility isn't based on health history for that product type.

## Why age matters so much for your quote

Age drives a large share of your pricing because it's the clearest available signal of overall risk from an actuarial standpoint. This is standard across the entire life insurance industry, not something specific to burial insurance or any one company. It's also why applying sooner rather than later tends to result in a better long-term rate — the age you lock in at application generally stays fixed for a level premium going forward.

## Why tobacco use is asked about separately

Tobacco use is tracked separately from general health questions because of its distinct, well-documented impact on life expectancy across large populations. Being upfront about tobacco use, including vaping or other nicotine products if the specific product asks about them, ensures your quote reflects an accurate rate class from the start.

## Why coverage amount is worth thinking through beforehand

You don't need an exact number before requesting a quote, but having a rough range in mind — "somewhere around $10,000 to $15,000" — helps you see genuinely useful numbers rather than a generic overview. Many people find it useful to compare two or three amounts side by side rather than committing to just one during the initial quote.

## What you don't need

**A funeral home selected.** The benefit is paid to your beneficiary as cash, not earmarked for a specific provider, so there's no need to have this decided in advance.

**Medical records.** Simplified issue applications are based on your answers to health questions, not a records review, and guaranteed issue doesn't involve health questions at all.

**A financial advisor or agent present.** The entire process is built to be self-service, though there's nothing wrong with looping someone in if you'd find that helpful.

## How the information actually gets used

Your age and tobacco status determine your baseline rate class. Health question answers, for simplified issue, refine that further. Coverage amount determines the actual premium within that rate class. None of this is mysterious — it's the same handful of factors used across the life insurance industry, just applied to a smaller, more predictable coverage amount than a traditional policy.

## A note on keeping your information secure

Reputable final expense insurance platforms handle personal information the way any legitimate financial services company should — with standard encryption and privacy practices consistent across the industry. Before entering any information online, it's reasonable to look for basic signs of legitimacy: a secure connection, clear information about the company behind the platform, and no pressure to provide more than what's actually needed for a quote. None of this should feel like a leap of faith if the platform is doing things properly.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need my Social Security number just to get a quote?**
No — that's typically only needed if you move forward to a formal application, not for an initial quote.

**What if I don't know my exact coverage amount yet?**
That's completely normal — comparing a couple of different amounts is a standard, useful part of the quote process.

**Is my information kept private if I don't apply?**
Requesting a quote is informational, and you're never obligated to apply just because you looked at your options.

**Can I get a quote without providing contact information?**
Most online quote tools ask for at least basic contact information to show you personalized numbers, though this doesn't commit you to anything further.

**How accurate is the quote compared to my final rate if I apply?**
Your quote reflects the information you provide; if your final application answers differ, your rate class could shift slightly, which is why accuracy upfront helps.

## What final expense insurance actually is

Final expense insurance is a small, permanent life insurance policy. It's not the giant $500,000 policy your financial advisor might push for income replacement — it's sized specifically to cover the stuff that piles up when someone passes away: funeral costs, burial or cremation, outstanding medical bills, maybe a little left over for the family.

Coverage amounts typically run from **$5,000 to $25,000**, and because the amount is smaller, the premiums are a lot friendlier than you'd expect from "real" life insurance.

## Why this matters more in Florida specifically

Florida has one of the largest senior populations in the country, and Florida funeral costs aren't cheap — cremation and burial services here run in line with, or above, national averages depending on the county. A lot of Florida families get caught off guard by the bill.

That's the entire reason this coverage exists: so your family isn't scrambling to cover a $8,000-$12,000 funeral bill during the worst week of their lives.

## How it works (the short version)

1. **Answer a few questions.** Age, some basic health questions (or none at all, depending on the product).
2. **Compare your options.** Different coverage amounts, different monthly costs.
3. **Apply online.** Most Florida residents can complete this from their couch.
4. **Get covered.** Some eligible applicants get a decision quickly — though this depends on your health answers, the product, and the carrier's underwriting.

## Do you need a medical exam?

For most final expense products, no. There are generally two paths:

| Type | Health questions? | Medical exam? | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Simplified issue | A short list, yes | No | Pricing depends on your answers |
| Guaranteed issue | None | No | Easier approval, may cost more per dollar of coverage |

Guaranteed issue policies often include something called a graded death benefit for the first two to three years — meaning if death occurs from natural causes in that early window, beneficiaries typically get back premiums paid (often with interest) rather than the full amount. Accidental death is usually covered in full from day one. It's a tradeoff for skipping health questions entirely, and it's worth knowing about upfront rather than finding out later.

## What does it actually cost?

Honestly? It depends — and anyone who gives you a flat number without asking your age is guessing. Real factors that move the number:

- **Your age** when you apply (younger = lower premium, generally)
- **Tobacco use**
- **Health history** (for simplified issue products)
- **Coverage amount** you choose
- **Which type of policy** you qualify for

## Common questions

**Is this the same as burial insurance?**
Basically, yes — "final expense" and "burial insurance" describe the same type of policy. We go deeper on that comparison [here](/blog/final-expense-vs-burial-insurance-florida/).

**Can I apply if I have health problems?**
Often, yes. Simplified issue products account for some health conditions in pricing, and guaranteed issue skips health questions altogether. [Florida Final Expense Insurance](/get-a-quote/) is currently available to Florida residents.

**How fast can I actually get covered?**
Some eligible applicants complete the whole thing — quote to coverage — in one sitting. It's not guaranteed for everyone (that depends on your answers and the carrier), but the process itself is built to be fast.

**What if I already have some life insurance?**
That's fine — final expense coverage typically stacks on top of whatever you already have. Plenty of people carry a small final expense policy specifically earmarked for funeral costs, separate from other coverage.

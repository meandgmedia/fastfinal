Married couples researching final expense insurance often assume there should be a single, combined policy covering both spouses — similar to how a lot of other household expenses get bundled together. In practice, individual policies are far more common in this category, and understanding why can help you make a more informed decision.

## Are joint final expense policies even available?

Joint final expense policies do exist in the market, though they're less common than individual policies. When available, they typically cover two people under a single policy, often paying out upon the first death, the second death, or in some structures, a portion at each.

## Why individual policies are more common and often more practical

**Simpler underwriting.** Since final expense insurance often depends on age and sometimes health, evaluating two people separately is generally more straightforward than combining two different risk profiles into one policy.

**Different coverage needs.** One spouse may want a different coverage amount than the other, based on their own preferences or what they specifically want covered — individual policies allow that flexibility naturally.

**Independent timing.** With individual policies, each spouse's coverage remains fully intact regardless of what happens with the other's policy, avoiding any complications tied to a shared structure.

## A comparison of the two approaches

| | Individual policies | Joint policy |
|---|---|---|
| Availability | Widely available | Less common |
| Coverage amount flexibility | Fully independent per person | Often shared or structured together |
| Underwriting | Separate for each person | Combined, can be more complex |
| Simplicity if one spouse passes | Other spouse's coverage unaffected | Depends on specific policy structure |

## Why most couples end up choosing individual policies

Even when a joint option is technically available, many couples find that two individual policies — each sized to that person's specific final expense needs — end up being simpler to understand, simpler to manage, and more flexible than a combined structure. It also means each spouse's coverage is locked in at their own age and health profile, rather than being tied to a joint evaluation that might not reflect either person's individual situation as accurately.

## What to think through as a couple

1. **Does each of you want the same coverage amount, or different amounts?** Individual policies handle this naturally; a joint policy may not.
2. **Do you want coverage to remain fully independent if one spouse passes first?** Individual policies generally provide this clearly.
3. **Are you looking for the simplest, most straightforward option?** For most couples, two individual policies tend to be easier to understand and manage over time.

## How to approach getting quotes as a couple

There's nothing stopping you from getting quotes for both spouses at the same time, even if you end up with two separate policies. This lets you compare coverage amounts and pricing for both of you side by side, making the whole process more efficient than handling it separately at different times.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is it cheaper to get one joint policy than two individual ones?**
Not necessarily — cost depends on the specific coverage amounts, ages, and health factors involved, which is why comparing real quotes matters more than assuming either structure is automatically cheaper.

**Can we choose different coverage amounts for each spouse?**
Yes, easily, with individual policies — this is one of the clearest advantages over a combined structure.

**What happens to my policy if my spouse passes away first?**
With individual policies, your own coverage is completely unaffected by anything that happens with your spouse's separate policy.

**Do we need to apply together, or can we apply separately?**
You can apply together or separately — there's no requirement to submit applications at the same time, though doing so can streamline the comparison process.

**Is a joint policy ever the better choice?**
In certain specific situations it can simplify things, but for most couples, individual policies offer more flexibility and clarity, especially regarding differing coverage needs.

"No medical exam" sounds like a marketing gimmick the first time you hear it. Fair reaction — a lot of insurance marketing *is* fluff. But this one's actually real, and it's worth understanding how it works rather than just taking it at face value.

## Wait, so there's really no exam?

Correct. No nurse coming to your house, no blood draw, no waiting on lab results. For final expense insurance specifically, this isn't the exception — it's closer to the norm.

There are two flavors of "no exam" coverage, and the difference matters:

### Simplified issue

You'll answer a short list of health questions — things like recent hospitalizations or major diagnoses. No exam, but your answers do affect your eligibility and your price.

### Guaranteed issue

No health questions *at all*. Your age and Florida residency are basically the whole conversation. The tradeoff: it usually costs more per dollar of coverage, and often includes a graded death benefit for the first two to three years (more on that below).

## What's a graded death benefit, actually?

This is the part people skip over and then get confused about later, so let's be direct about it. On many guaranteed issue policies, if death happens from natural causes within the first two or three years, your beneficiary doesn't get the full coverage amount — they typically get back the premiums paid, often with interest. Accidental death is usually covered in full from day one.

After that grading period ends, the policy pays the full amount for any reason. It's not a trick, it's just how guaranteed issue policies manage risk since they skip health questions entirely.

## Who actually benefits from no-exam coverage?

- Florida seniors who don't want to deal with scheduling a medical exam
- Anyone with a health condition that made traditional life insurance a hassle
- People who just want this handled quickly, without a multi-week underwriting process
- Applicants who got declined elsewhere and assumed that was the final word (usually isn't)

## Does skipping the exam mean it's not "real" insurance?

Not remotely. It's a real, regulated life insurance policy — it's just structured differently to make approval faster and simpler. The coverage amount is smaller (typically $5,000–$25,000) because that's what final expense insurance is designed for in the first place.

## How to know which type fits you

Honestly, the easiest way is to just get a quote and see what you're eligible for — simplified issue pricing is usually more favorable if you qualify, and guaranteed issue is there as a safety net if health questions are a concern. You don't have to figure this out theoretically; the quote process will show you.

## Frequently asked questions

**Will I definitely get approved with guaranteed issue?**
Guaranteed issue means no health questions, but eligibility can still depend on factors like age and Florida residency. It's not automatic for literally everyone, but it's built to be accessible to most applicants.

**Is guaranteed issue always more expensive?**
Generally, yes, per dollar of coverage — you're trading a bit of cost for skipping health questions entirely.

**Can I switch from guaranteed issue to simplified issue later?**
Not really how it works — you'd apply for a new policy if your health situation changes and you want to explore simplified issue pricing.

**How fast is the process?**
Fast. Some eligible applicants complete the whole thing online in one sitting.

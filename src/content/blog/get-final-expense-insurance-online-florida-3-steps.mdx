There was a time when buying life insurance meant scheduling an in-home visit, sitting through a sales pitch at your kitchen table, and waiting weeks for paperwork. That time is mostly over — at least for final expense insurance in Florida.

Here's what the actual process looks like now, broken into three steps, because it really is that simple.

## Step 1: Get your quote

You answer a handful of questions — your age, maybe some basic health questions depending on the product, and how much coverage you're thinking about. That's it. No home visit, no phone call required to just *see numbers*.

This step takes most people under five minutes.

## Step 2: Compare your options

Once you've got your quote, you'll see what's actually available to you — different coverage amounts, different monthly costs. This is the part where you get to actually think it through instead of being pressured into a decision on the spot.

A few things worth comparing side by side:

- **Coverage amount** — does it realistically cover what you want it to?
- **Monthly premium** — is it something you're comfortable paying long-term?
- **Policy type** — simplified issue (health questions, no exam) vs. guaranteed issue (no health questions at all)

## Step 3: Apply and get covered

If you like what you see, you complete the application online. Depending on your answers and the product, some eligible applicants get a decision quickly — sometimes in the same sitting. It's not a guarantee for everyone (that depends on underwriting), but the *process* itself is built for speed, not for making you wait around.

## Why doing this online actually works better

- **No pressure.** You're not sitting across from a salesperson.
- **No scheduling.** You do it whenever — 9am or 11pm, doesn't matter.
- **You can actually compare.** It's easier to think clearly about numbers on a screen than in a conversation.
- **It's honest.** Everything you need to know is right there in front of you, not buried in a pitch.

## What you'll want to have ready

Nothing complicated. Just:

- Your basic info (name, date of birth, contact details)
- A general sense of the coverage amount you're considering
- Honest answers to any health questions asked (accuracy matters for simplified issue pricing)

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need to talk to anyone on the phone?**
No — the whole thing is designed to be self-service. If you'd rather not use the phone at all, you don't have to.

**Is my information secure?**
Reputable final expense platforms handle your information the way any financial services company should — this is standard practice across the industry.

**What if I start and don't finish?**
No obligation, ever. Getting a quote doesn't commit you to anything.

**How is this different from calling an agent?**
Same underlying products, different experience. You're getting the same coverage options without the sales call.

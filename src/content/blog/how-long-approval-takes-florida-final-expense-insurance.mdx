"How long does this actually take" is one of the most practical questions people ask before starting a final expense insurance application, and it deserves a real, specific answer rather than a vague "it depends."

## The honest range

For many applicants, especially those applying for guaranteed issue, approval can happen within the same day — sometimes within the same sitting as the application. For simplified issue applicants, the timeline can be similarly fast, though it depends more on the specifics of the health questions and answers involved. It's not universal for every single applicant, but same-day or near-same-day approval is a realistic, common outcome.

## What happens at each stage

**Getting your quote:** A few minutes. This is the fastest part of the entire process, requiring just basic information about your age, tobacco use, and desired coverage amount.

**Completing the application:** Roughly 10 to 15 minutes for most applicants, including answering health questions if applicable.

**Underwriting review:** This is where timing varies most. Guaranteed issue applications, since they skip health questions, often move through this stage almost immediately. Simplified issue applications involve a review of your health answers, which can take anywhere from minutes to a bit longer depending on the complexity of your situation.

## Factors that speed things up

- **Applying for guaranteed issue**, which removes health question review from the timeline entirely
- **Providing complete, accurate information the first time**, avoiding any need for follow-up
- **Applying online rather than by phone**, since digital applications typically process faster

## Factors that can add time

- **More complex health histories** for simplified issue applicants, which may require additional review
- **Incomplete or inconsistent application information**, which can trigger a request for clarification
- **Applying for a product you don't ultimately qualify for**, requiring a restart with a different option

## Why the timeline matters beyond convenience

Beyond simply wanting an answer quickly, there's a real financial reason speed matters here: age is one of the biggest pricing factors in final expense insurance, and it only moves in one direction. A faster approval process means locking in your rate at today's age rather than the process dragging into a period where pricing shifts against you.

## Setting realistic expectations for your specific situation

If speed is your top priority, guaranteed issue is generally the more predictable path, since it removes the one variable — health question review — most likely to extend the timeline. If you're comfortable with a likely-but-not-certain fast outcome, simplified issue remains a strong option, especially if it comes with more favorable pricing for your situation.

## What to do if your application is taking longer than expected

This doesn't necessarily indicate a problem. It can simply mean your specific answers require a closer look, which is a normal part of the process for some applicants. If you're concerned, reaching out directly to the carrier or platform you applied through is the most reliable way to get a status update, rather than guessing.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I get approved in under an hour?**
For some applicants, particularly guaranteed issue applicants, yes — though this isn't guaranteed universally and depends on individual circumstances.

**Does applying at a certain time of day affect approval speed?**
This can vary by carrier's processing hours, but many online applications process automatically regardless of when you apply.

**Is a longer approval time a bad sign?**
Not necessarily — it often just reflects additional review for your specific answers rather than any issue with your application.

**Can I check my application status while waiting?**
Most platforms provide some way to check status, though the specific method varies — check with whichever platform you applied through.

**Does a fast approval mean less thorough underwriting?**
No — the speed reflects the product's simplified underwriting design, not a lower standard of review.

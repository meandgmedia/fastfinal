One of the first questions most people ask about final expense insurance is simple: what's it going to cost? The honest answer is "it depends" — but that's not a dodge. Final expense premiums are based on a handful of specific, well-understood factors, and once you understand them, it's much easier to make sense of any quote you receive.

This article breaks down what actually drives the price of a final expense policy, walks through example coverage amounts, and explains why two people can get very different quotes for what looks like the same coverage.

## What determines the price of final expense insurance?

Final expense insurance pricing comes down to a combination of who you are, what you're buying, and which type of policy you choose. Here's how each factor plays in.

### Age

Age is one of the biggest factors in your premium. In general, the younger you are when you apply, the lower your premium is likely to be — and because final expense premiums are usually designed to stay level for life, locking in a rate at a younger age can mean paying less over the long run compared to waiting.

### Gender

Life insurance pricing, including final expense insurance, often differs between male and female applicants. This is standard across the life insurance industry and reflects actuarial data on life expectancy, rather than anything specific to any one carrier's product.

### Health

For simplified issue policies, your answers to a short list of health questions affect both your eligibility and your rate class. Conditions like heart disease, diabetes, or a recent hospitalization can move you into a different pricing tier — or, in some cases, point you toward a guaranteed issue product instead. Guaranteed issue policies skip health questions entirely, which simplifies approval but usually comes at a higher cost per dollar of coverage.

### Tobacco use

Tobacco use is one of the most consistent rating factors across life insurance products. Applicants who use tobacco typically pay more than non-tobacco applicants for the same coverage amount, reflecting the statistical impact tobacco use has on life expectancy.

### Coverage amount

This one's straightforward: more coverage costs more. A $25,000 policy will cost more than a $5,000 policy, all else being equal. The question worth spending time on isn't "what's the cheapest option" but "what amount actually covers what I want it to cover" — which is a good reason to compare a few coverage tiers rather than defaulting to the smallest one.

### Policy type

As covered in our guide to [what final expense insurance is](/blog/what-is-final-expense-insurance/), final expense products generally fall into two categories:

- **Simplified issue** policies ask health questions but skip the medical exam. Pricing reflects your answers.
- **Guaranteed issue** policies ask no health questions. They're easier to qualify for but often cost more per dollar of coverage, and may include a graded death benefit for the first two to three years.

Two applicants of the same age and gender can end up with meaningfully different premiums simply because one qualifies for simplified issue pricing and the other needs a guaranteed issue product.

## Understanding rate classes

Within simplified issue products, carriers typically sort applicants into a small number of rate classes based on their health answers. While the exact names and criteria vary by carrier, you'll commonly see categories along these lines:

- **Preferred or Level** — for applicants in relatively good health, with no major conditions flagged in the health questions. This tier generally gets the most favorable pricing and full benefits from day one.
- **Standard** — for applicants with some health history that doesn't disqualify them from simplified issue but moves them out of the top pricing tier.
- **Graded or Modified** — sometimes offered to applicants whose health history doesn't qualify for standard simplified issue pricing, but who don't need a fully guaranteed issue product either. These policies often include a graded death benefit similar to guaranteed issue policies, where full coverage phases in over the first two to three years.

Where you land in these tiers has a direct effect on your premium — which is part of why final expense pricing can look so different from one applicant to the next, even with identical coverage amounts.

## Questions worth asking when comparing quotes

Once you have a few quotes in hand, a handful of questions can help you compare them on equal footing rather than just looking at the monthly premium:

- **Is this simplified issue or guaranteed issue?** The two aren't directly comparable on price alone, since guaranteed issue trades a higher cost for easier qualification.
- **Is there a graded death benefit, and if so, for how long?** Know whether your beneficiary would receive the full face amount or a return of premiums if death occurs from natural causes during the early years of the policy.
- **Is the premium guaranteed to stay level?** Most final expense policies are designed this way, but it's worth confirming rather than assuming.
- **What's excluded, if anything?** Understand any exclusions or waiting periods before you compare price alone.
- **Is the coverage amount enough for what I actually want it to cover?** A lower premium on a coverage amount that doesn't meet your family's needs isn't necessarily the better deal.

Comparing quotes side by side on these points — not just the bottom-line premium — is the best way to know you're actually comparing similar coverage.

## Example coverage amounts and what they're often used for

Because exact pricing depends on so many individual factors, it's more useful to think in terms of coverage amount tiers than to look for a single "average price." Here are common coverage amounts and how people often think about them:

| Coverage amount | Commonly considered for |
|---|---|
| $5,000 | A basic cremation or simple service |
| $10,000 | A more complete funeral service |
| $15,000 | Funeral costs with some cushion for other expenses |
| $20,000 | Funeral costs plus outstanding bills |
| $25,000 | Broader final expense coverage |

These figures are general examples meant to help you think through your own needs — not a quote, and not a guarantee of what any specific policy will cost. Actual premiums depend on the factors above and the specific carrier and product you choose.

## Why do quotes differ so much from person to person?

If you've compared notes with a friend or family member who also has a final expense policy, you may have noticed your quotes look nothing alike — even for similar coverage amounts. That's expected, and it usually comes down to a combination of:

- **Different ages at the time of application**
- **Different health histories**, especially for simplified issue products
- **Different policy types** (simplified issue vs. guaranteed issue)
- **Different carriers**, each with their own underwriting guidelines and pricing
- **Different coverage amounts**, even if they look similar at a glance

None of this means one quote is "wrong" and another is "right" — it just reflects how individualized life insurance pricing actually is. This is also why generic online cost calculators or rules of thumb ("final expense insurance costs about $X per month") are only loosely useful. They can give you a rough sense of scale, but they can't account for your specific age, health, and eligibility.

## A few tips for keeping costs manageable

If affordability is your main concern, a few practical approaches can help:

- **Apply sooner rather than later.** Since age is one of the biggest pricing factors, and final expense premiums are typically level for life, applying earlier can lock in a lower rate for the long term.
- **Be accurate on health questions.** For simplified issue policies, giving accurate answers helps you get placed in the right rate class from the start, which avoids issues later.
- **Compare coverage amounts, not just one number.** Sometimes a slightly lower coverage amount brings the premium down to a much more comfortable level without leaving your family underinsured for a typical funeral.
- **Compare more than one product.** Simplified issue and guaranteed issue policies can price very differently for people in different health situations — comparing both can reveal real savings.

## The bottom line

Final expense insurance pricing isn't arbitrary — it's driven by a specific, understandable set of factors: your age, gender, health, tobacco use, the coverage amount you choose, and the type of policy you qualify for.
